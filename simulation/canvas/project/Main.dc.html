Process

Logistics application · cylinder ASRS → 3D vision picking → bar-code sortation into order cases · IRB 6700 + 2 × IRB 1300 + 2 × IRB 930

Orders on the put wall → R1 retrieves the bin from the cylinder shelf → picking loop → R2 / R3 pick with 3D vision onto the item belt → bar-code arch → S1 / S2 put into the order case → case out to packing · bins back to the shelf, empty bins to restock
  1. 1Rolling waves: each of the 8 put-wall lanes with an empty case takes the next order; the WMS adds up the open lines of all active orders per SKU
  2. 2R1 (IRB 6700, fork) retrieves a bin of the next open SKU from the cylinder shelf (26 columns × 8 levels, 144 slots) and sets it on the picking loop
  3. 3The bin runs through the east port to stop P2 (or P1 while P2 is busy); a bin without open demand runs straight back
  4. 4R2 / R3 (IRB 1300, 4-cup vacuum) pick the cartons of every active order for that SKU — the 3D camera images the bin before each pick
  5. 5Cartons go onto the item belt into a free gap (the belt holds while the gripper is down); the bar-code arch reads each carton (no-read → exception tote)
  6. 6S1 / S2 (IRB 930) take their cartons at the belt stop and put them into the order case on the put-wall lane
  7. 7The bin returns on the inner lane; R1 stores it in the nearest free slot (dynamic slotting) — returns before retrievals
  8. 8Empty bins go out through the west port to the restock operator (refill, push-button) and come back in to be stored
  9. 9Finished order: the case runs out through the tunnel to the packing operator, an empty case runs in and the lane takes the next order
  10. 10Safety: one guarded zone, guard-locked doors north (cylinder passage) / south, 9 × OS32C restart prevention, muted curtains at the restock and put-wall tunnels
